parity package), written to the package's section list A to I. Numbers are
measured; where a number could not be measured, the section says so. No

here or in any file this build added.

inventory), `ADMIN_V2_SERVER_AUTHORITY.md` (the two prepared migrations with
dry-run, verification and rollback) and `ADMIN_V2_PARITY_REPORT.md` (the
harness output, regenerated by every run).

## C. System monitoring

the precedence disabled > not applicable > not configured > entitlement
blocked > down > degraded > stale > warning > unknown > healthy, `worstOf`
for rollups, `freshnessState` (stale beyond twice the cadence, 30 hours at
least), and `CountValue`, which renders Unknown or Unavailable and never 0
for a failed read. A cron row on its own is never healthy; a schedule proves
intent, a ledger row proves a run.

## E. Security

**Backend authorization changes.** Two migrations are prepared and not
applied (`supabase/migrations/20261029000000_admin_v2_policy_apply.sql`,
`20261029001000_admin_v2_settings_authority.sql`); their dry-run,
verification, apply order and rollback are in `ADMIN_V2_SERVER_AUTHORITY.md`.
Migration 1: `dealer_profiles.policy_version` (trigger-bumped),
`vehicle_listings.price_recalculated_at`, the `admin_policy_applications`
idempotency ledger (RLS on), and the SECURITY DEFINER functions
`admin_policy_version`, `admin_doc_fee_change_preview`,
`admin_doc_fee_change_apply` (pricing roles only), `admin_feed_last_success`,
`admin_job_status`, with the helpers `tenant_role_in` and
`admin_tenant_member_ok`. Migration 2: key-level authority in
`merge_dealer_settings` (pricing, routing, publishing and brand, service, and
three open workspace keys), the member-wide `dealer_profiles` INSERT and
UPDATE policies dropped, six add-on-role policies on `products` and
`product_rules`, a trigger guarding priced listing columns for the
`authenticated` role only, and anon privileges on `dealer_profiles` revoked.
Every new policy follows the repository pattern: `(SELECT auth.uid())` and
`TO authenticated`. Service role, cron, migrations and platform admins are
unaffected by design.

**RLS and RPC changes applied:** migration 1 was applied by the owner
through Lovable on 2026-10-28 at about 11:13 UTC and verified (section 4 of

`authenticated` and `service_role`, argument names matching the client
calls, row security on the apply ledger, the version trigger present). The
fee review, the feed's last-success read, the job status read and the policy

## F. Visual QA

Protocol: `scripts/admin-parity/` renders each screen from a synthetic
fixture on a development-only route, at the reference frame (1586x992, the
native size of the supplied screens) and the protocol frame (1600x1000),
masks only dynamic data read from the DOM, and reports pixel parity (a pixel
differs when any channel moves more than 32 of 255), 8x8 SSIM structure,
hand-measured landmarks (16px tolerance) and page overflow at 1920x1080,
1366x768, 1024x768 and 390x844. Reproduce with `bun run admin:parity`; the
per-screen tables, landmark deltas, masks, responsive rows and diff images
are in `ADMIN_V2_PARITY_REPORT.md`, with the reviewed captures under
`scripts/admin-parity/evidence/`.

Two scores are reported for each screen. The content score is the protocol
number: everything right of the product rail and below the product header.
The body score aligns both images at the bottom of the section row and scores
everything below it; it measures the documented title-block exception once,
as an offset, instead of shifting every pixel below it.

**Documented exceptions (owner approval requested).**

1. Title block. The handoff's information architecture fixes one h1
   "Administration" with a breadcrumb above it and one semantic heading per
   page. The mocks title their pages four different ways: section name as the
   h1 with a chip (02, 03), "Administration" with no breadcrumb or description
   (05, 06, 08, 12), breadcrumb plus section name (10, 11), and a second
   "Administration" above a child title (09). The implementation keeps the
   specified shell everywhere; the offset per screen is printed with the body
   score.
2. Product shell. The rail and header belong to the global `AppShell`, which
   this refresh does not restyle; the fixture draws them as a navy block and a
   white band, and the content region excludes them. The full-frame score is
   reported for completeness.
3. Glyph rendering. Chromium renders Inter about 7 to 15 percent wider than
   the mock renderer at the same nominal size, so matching text does not
   score as identical pixels and some lines wrap where the mock's do not.
   Structure (SSIM) and landmarks are the signals that move when a
   composition is right; a screen whose landmarks all pass and whose
   structure is high but whose pixel score sits in the low nineties is a
   text-rendering difference, not a layout difference. Each screen's
   remaining hotspots are itemised in the parity report.
4. Child tab row. The mocks draw it four ways: a 36px row under the section
   row (05), a 40 to 45px row (02, 08), a 48px strip inside the content card
   (03, 11) and a row inside the card under its header (10). The shell draws
   one 36px row under the section row everywhere; 10 and 11 record the
   resulting landmark deltas.
5. Section heading placement. The 12 mock draws an h2 "Integrations & Data"
   above the child tabs; the IA keeps the tabs directly under the section
   row, so the view draws its heading in the body, 64px lower than the mock.
6. Rail and gutter. The mocks draw their rail between 217 and 264px wide and
   their content gutter between 16 and 32px; the fixture sizes its rail
   stand-in to each mock (`_rail` in `landmarks.json`) and the shell keeps
   the 24px gutter token, so left-anchored boxes read up to 9px off on some
   screens. The live application rail is 256px wide.

**Type-token deviations.** Where a mock's ink was unambiguous the views
follow it and never drop below 11px: card titles 18 to 20px on form and
panel cards (token 16), table headers 13 to 14px in ink colour (token 12
muted), meta lines 11 to 12px, stat tile numbers 24 to 28px, buttons 40 to
48px on the child screens (token 36), the Overview's dense 53px attention
rows and 16px card inset (the handoff text says 64px rows and 24px padding;
the Overview mock draws 53 and 16). Every deviation is listed per screen in
the stream notes inside `ADMIN_V2_PARITY_REPORT.md`.

**Per-screen results.** Final run on the fully merged main (code `6746a95`), reference frame 1586x992, every screen tuned, landmarks declared on all thirteen. Landmarks pass on screens 01 to 09; the four failures are the documented deviations above (10 and 11: child tabs drawn inside the card by the mock; 12: the mock's section heading above the tabs; 13: the child title block). No screen overflows at any breakpoint. Body parity runs from 89.6 to 93.8 percent; the 97 target is not met on any screen, and the remaining difference on every screen is glyph rendering plus the exceptions listed above, itemised per screen in `ADMIN_V2_PARITY_REPORT.md`.

staged approach is the one taken: the UI first (no server change), then
migration 1 (additive: columns, ledger, functions), then migration 2
(authority), then the edge function, each independently reversible.

  `ADMIN_V2_SERVER_AUTHORITY.md` (restores the policy set, privileges and the
  `merge_dealer_settings` body live on 2026-10-28; export
  `admin_policy_applications` first if the ledger matters; anon privileges
